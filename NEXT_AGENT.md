## The goal, in the owner's words: extreme, lifelike realism

The reference is a modern architectural-visualisation interior render. The *style and realism*
matter, not the specific objects. Judge every screenshot against a photo, not against "good for
WebGL". The owner has repeatedly rejected results that were merely nice. Specifically:

1. **Nothing flat or generic.** Walls, floors and ceilings need relief, varied sheen, wear and
   reflections. A tiled pattern on a flat plane is a failure.
2. **Objects must have real shape.** No furniture made of plain boxes. Real chairs, desks, lamps
   and sofas have curves, contours, soft edges, seams, indents, stitching, feet, fixings and
   several different materials each. Model those.
3. **Light must be soft and believable.** Indirect light, ambient occlusion, area lamps, warm
   concealed LEDs, sun patches. Use the GPU light bake (`bake.ts`) for anything static.
4. **Fine detail everywhere**: high-resolution generated textures, micro-bevels, material
   variation. If a surface looks computer-made up close, improve it.
